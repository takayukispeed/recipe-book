# Pork Gyoza

Pan-fried Japanese dumplings with store-bought wrappers: crisp on the bottom, steamed on top. Makes enough filling for one full package of wrappers, about 50.

## Ingredients

### Filling

- 1½ lb (680 g) ground pork, not extra-lean
- 6 green onions, finely chopped
- 3 cloves garlic, grated
- 1½ Tbsp (22 mL) grated fresh ginger
- 1½ Tbsp (22 mL) soy sauce
- 1½ Tbsp (22 mL) sake, or dry sherry
- 1 Tbsp (15 mL) toasted sesame oil
- 1½ tsp (7 mL) sugar
- 1½ tsp (7 mL) potato starch or cornstarch
- ½ tsp (2 mL) salt
- ½ tsp (2 mL) white pepper
- 3 Tbsp (45 mL) cold water or chicken stock

### Wrapping and cooking

- 1 package round gyoza wrappers (about 50), thawed if frozen
- 3 Tbsp (45 mL) neutral oil, for frying
- 1 tsp (5 mL) toasted sesame oil, to finish
- Water, for sealing and steaming

### Dipping sauce

- 3 Tbsp (45 mL) soy sauce
- 2 Tbsp (30 mL) rice vinegar
- Rāyu (Japanese chili oil), to taste

## Method

1. **Make the filling:** in a bowl, combine the pork, garlic, ginger, soy sauce, sake, sesame oil, sugar, starch, salt and white pepper. Stir hard in one direction, or knead by hand, for 1–2 min until the mixture turns sticky and paste-like. Add the cold water or stock a tablespoon at a time, stirring until each addition is absorbed. With no vegetables in the filling, this is what keeps it juicy. Mix in the green onions.
2. **Set up:** have a small bowl of water and a parchment-lined tray ready. Keep the stack of wrappers under a damp towel so they don't dry out.
3. **Fill and fold:** put about 1 Tbsp of filling in the centre of a wrapper. Wet the edge halfway round with a fingertip, then fold the wrapper in half over the filling. Pleat the front edge only, making 5–6 small pleats, and press each one against the flat back edge to seal. Stand each gyoza on the tray, flat side down, under a damp towel. Repeat.
4. **Fry:** heat 1 Tbsp oil in a large non-stick skillet with a lid over medium-high heat. Arrange gyoza flat side down in tight rows or a ring, just touching. Fry 2–3 min, until the bottoms are deep golden.
5. **Steam:** pour in about ⅓ cup (80 mL) water. It will spit, so stand back. Cover immediately, lower to medium and steam 3–4 min, until the wrappers turn translucent and the filling is cooked through.
6. **Crisp:** uncover and let the remaining water boil off. Drizzle a little of the sesame oil around the edge and fry 1–2 min more, until the bottoms are crisp again and the gyoza slide when you shake the pan.
7. **Serve:** slide them onto a plate or invert the pan onto one, crisp side up. Mix the soy sauce and rice vinegar, add chili oil to taste, and serve right away. Wipe the pan and repeat with the remaining oil and gyoza.

## Notes

- **Sized for one package:** about 800 g of filling in total, or about 16 g (1 Tbsp) per gyoza. Package counts vary by brand, roughly 40 to 60. If yours is on the small side, put a little more in each, or freeze any leftover filling for the next batch.
- **To freeze:** freeze uncooked gyoza in a single layer on the tray, then bag them. Cook from frozen, adding 1–2 min to the steaming.
- **Crispy "wings":** stir 1 tsp flour into the steaming water. It sets into a lacy crust joining the gyoza.
- The cooking amounts in steps 4–7 are per batch: about 1 Tbsp (15 mL) oil and ⅓ cup (80 mL) water for 15–20 gyoza in a 10–12 inch pan.
