# Pork Gyoza

Pan-fried Japanese dumplings with store-bought wrappers: crisp on the bottom, steamed on top.

## Ingredients

### Filling

- 1 lb (450 g) ground pork, not extra-lean
- 2 cups (150 g) finely chopped napa cabbage
- ½ tsp (2 mL) salt, for the cabbage
- 4 green onions, finely chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, grated
- 1 Tbsp (15 mL) grated fresh ginger
- 1 Tbsp (15 mL) soy sauce
- 1 Tbsp (15 mL) sake, or dry sherry
- 2 tsp (10 mL) toasted sesame oil
- 1 tsp (5 mL) sugar
- 1 tsp (5 mL) potato starch or cornstarch
- ¼ tsp (1 mL) white pepper

### Wrapping and cooking

- 40 round gyoza wrappers (about 1 package), thawed if frozen
- 2 Tbsp (30 mL) neutral oil, for frying
- 1 tsp (5 mL) toasted sesame oil, to finish
- Water, for sealing and steaming

### Dipping sauce

- 3 Tbsp (45 mL) soy sauce
- 2 Tbsp (30 mL) rice vinegar
- Rāyu (Japanese chili oil), to taste

## Method

1. **Salt the cabbage:** toss the cabbage with the ½ tsp salt and leave 10 min. Wrap it in a clean tea towel and squeeze out as much water as you can. Wet cabbage makes soggy, leaky gyoza.
2. **Make the filling:** in a bowl, combine the pork, garlic, ginger, soy sauce, sake, sesame oil, sugar, starch and white pepper. Stir hard in one direction, or knead by hand, for 1–2 min until the mixture turns sticky and paste-like. This keeps the filling juicy. Mix in the cabbage and green onions.
3. **Set up:** have a small bowl of water and a parchment-lined tray ready. Keep the stack of wrappers under a damp towel so they don't dry out.
4. **Fill and fold:** put about 1 Tbsp of filling in the centre of a wrapper. Wet the edge halfway round with a fingertip, then fold the wrapper in half over the filling. Pleat the front edge only, making 5–6 small pleats, and press each one against the flat back edge to seal. Stand each gyoza on the tray, flat side down, under a damp towel. Repeat.
5. **Fry:** heat 1 Tbsp oil in a large non-stick skillet with a lid over medium-high heat. Arrange gyoza flat side down in tight rows or a ring, just touching. Fry 2–3 min, until the bottoms are deep golden.
6. **Steam:** pour in about ⅓ cup (80 mL) water. It will spit, so stand back. Cover immediately, lower to medium and steam 3–4 min, until the wrappers turn translucent and the filling is cooked through.
7. **Crisp:** uncover and let the remaining water boil off. Drizzle a little of the sesame oil around the edge and fry 1–2 min more, until the bottoms are crisp again and the gyoza slide when you shake the pan.
8. **Serve:** slide them onto a plate or invert the pan onto one, crisp side up. Mix the soy sauce and rice vinegar, add chili oil to taste, and serve right away. Wipe the pan and repeat with the remaining oil and gyoza.

## Notes

- **To freeze:** freeze uncooked gyoza in a single layer on the tray, then bag them. Cook from frozen, adding 1–2 min to the steaming.
- **Crispy "wings":** stir 1 tsp flour into the steaming water. It sets into a lacy crust joining the gyoza.
- The cooking amounts in steps 5–8 are per batch: about 1 Tbsp (15 mL) oil and ⅓ cup (80 mL) water for 15–20 gyoza in a 10–12 inch pan.
